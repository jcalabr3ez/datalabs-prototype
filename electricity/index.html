Which states pay the most?

The twelve highest all-sector average retail prices, plus Massachusetts if it is not already in that set. Massachusetts is marked in gold. The dashed line is the EIA U.S. Total. Every state is in the table below.
Figure 1 All-sector average retail electricity price by state
Price is cents per kilowatthour sold to ultimate customers, all sectors combined. Hawaii, Alaska, and the New England states sit at the top of the ranking because isolated or constrained grids and fuel mix show up in the all-sector average; the Plains and parts of the interior sit at the bottom. Rankings are among the 50 states and the District of Columbia (derived, SRC-401).
How this is calculated
All-sector average (EIA, not Pioneer)
price = total retail revenue / total retail sales The U.S. figure is EIA's published U.S. Total row: U.S. price = U.S. revenue / U.S. sales It is not the unweighted mean of the 51 state-and-D.C. prices.
Result: the cents-per-kWh figures published on this page are EIA's, rounded to two decimals as in Electric Power Annual table 2.10.
Inputs: EIA Form EIA-861 historical state file, Total Electric Industry, Total price column, checked against Electric Power Annual table 2.10. Calculation of ranks: Pioneer Institute.
Source: EIA Electric Power Annual table 2.10 and Form EIA-861 historical state file (SRC-401). Calculation: Pioneer Institute (ranks only).

How have prices moved since 2012?

Two lines: the EIA U.S. Total and one selected state. The series is the same all-sector average as Figure 1, year by year.
Figure 2 All-sector average retail price, United States and selected state
Massachusetts rose from 13.79 cents in 2012 to more than 23 cents in the latest years, a steeper climb than the national line (derived, SRC-401). The U.S. Total stayed in the low-to-mid teens across the same window. Select another state to compare it with the national series.
Source: EIA Form EIA-861 historical state file, Total Electric Industry (SRC-401), 2012 through the latest annual year. Calculation: Pioneer Institute.

How much electricity did each state sell?

Retail sales to ultimate customers, in million megawatthours, for the latest year. This is the volume behind the price: what was sold, not what a household paid on a bill.
Figure 3 Total retail electricity sales by state
Sales are megawatthours delivered to residential, commercial, industrial, and transportation customers combined (SRC-401). Large states lead because they have more customers, not because prices are higher. Per-capita sales, in the table, divide this volume by the Census July 1 resident population (derived, SRC-401, SRC-402).
Source: EIA Form EIA-861 historical state file, Total sales (SRC-401). Population: Census Bureau vintage estimates (SRC-402). Calculation: Pioneer Institute (per capita only).

How much did each state generate?

Net generation at utility-scale plants located in the state, all energy sources. Generation is not the same as sales: power crosses state lines, and this figure is where it was produced.
Figure 4 Net electricity generation by state
Net generation, Total Electric Power Industry, all energy sources (SRC-403). A state can sell more than it generates, or the reverse. Net summer capacity, in the table, is the EIA-860 all-sources total (SRC-404).
Source: EIA-923 annual generation by state (SRC-403); EIA-860 existing capacity (SRC-404). Calculation: Pioneer Institute (per capita only).

▶What this page does not cover

This tool publishes the all-sector average retail price by state, plus sales, generation, capacity, and per-capita figures where the population join is complete. It does not publish residential, commercial, or industrial prices as separate series, utility or city rates, forecasts, bill calculators, or rate-case advice. The U.S. figure is always EIA’s U.S. Total row. Questions on excluded subjects asked through the DataLabs front door are declined rather than improvised.
