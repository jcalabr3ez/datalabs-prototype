Enrollment, MCAS, attendance, dropouts, school finances, and Chapter 74 vocational-technical programs for Massachusetts districts.

What are the key takeaways?

Current expenditures per public-school pupil were $17,644 in the United States in fiscal year 2024 (SRC-606-01). Massachusetts spent $27,008, rank 5 of 51 (derived, SRC-606-01). Fall 2024 enrollment in Massachusetts was 915,932 (SRC-606-02). Chapter 74 career technical education enrolled 59,962 Massachusetts high-school students in 2025-26, 21.2 percent of high-school enrollment (SRC-606-03). That is 5,676 more students than in 2021-22 (derived, SRC-606-03). 95 districts and 111 schools reported a Chapter 74 program; 29 were regional vocational or agricultural districts (derived, SRC-606-03). Greater Lowell Regional Vocational Technical was the largest district at 2,312 (SRC-606-03). Grade-9 exploratory enrolled 17,147; the largest occupational program was Health Assisting at 3,617 (SRC-606-03). After Dark partnership enrollment was 406, a published subset of Chapter 74 (SRC-606-03). All college-and-career pathways together enrolled 82,903 (SRC-606-03). On the 2025 Next Generation MCAS, 42.0% of grades 3-8 students met or exceeded expectations in English language arts and 41.0% in mathematics (SRC-606-04). Grade 10 was 51.0% ELA and 45.0% math (SRC-606-04). The attendance rate was 93.2% in 2024-25; 18.8% of students were chronically absent (SRC-606-05). The high-school dropout rate was 1.8%, 5,143 students (SRC-606-06). Among districts, Truro had the highest total expenditures per pupil at $59,111 (SRC-606-07). Waitlists and lottery outcomes are not a published statewide table.
