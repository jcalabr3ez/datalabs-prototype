Transportation & MBTA

How many people ride the T, month by month and mode by mode, and what each trip costs to provide. Rebuilt from federal filings and verified against the National Transit Database.

What are the key takeaways?

Trips in May 2026
24.8M
The T carried 24,773,714 unlinked passenger trips in May 2026 (SRC-301), 77.7 percent of the same month in 2019 (derived vs same month 2019, SRC-301).
Why it matters: Ridership is growing again, but the system is still carrying roughly a fifth fewer trips than before the pandemic.
Source: FTA NTD Complete Monthly Ridership (SRC-301), fetched Aug 2, 2026
Fastest recovery
Commuter rail
Commuter rail stands at 101.3 percent of its May 2019 ridership, the only mode above its pre-pandemic level; the subway sits at 66.1 percent (derived, SRC-301).
Why it matters: The suburbs came back to the T before the urban core did, which changes where service and investment matter most.
Source: derived from SRC-301, month vs same month 2019
Direction
+4.1% YoY
May 2026 ridership is 4.1 percent above May 2025 (derived, SRC-301), and monthly totals have topped 25 million three times since October 2024 (SRC-301).
Why it matters: The recovery is still compounding rather than stalled, but at this pace a full return to 2019 remains years away.
Source: FTA NTD Complete Monthly Ridership (SRC-301)

Is the T back to pre-pandemic ridership?

One line answers it: every trip taken on the system, month by month, since 2014. The pandemic crater is the deepest point in the record, and the climb back is real but incomplete. The dashed line marks the 2019 monthly average, the level the system has not yet reached again.
Figure 1 MBTA monthly ridership, all modes, 2014 to May 2026
Unlinked passenger trips across every MBTA mode. April 2020, the pandemic floor, saw 4,297,427 trips, about 13.7 percent of the same month a year earlier (derived, SRC-301). The recovery has been steady rather than sharp: 24,773,714 trips in May 2026 against 31,879,535 in May 2019 puts the system at 77.7 percent of its pre-pandemic level (derived vs same month 2019, SRC-301).
How this is calculated
Recovery vs same month 2019 (the measure used throughout this page)
recovery = month / same month 2019 x 100 May 2026: 24,773,714 / 31,879,535 x 100 = 77.7% year over year: 24,773,714 / 23,796,675 x 100 - 100 = +4.1%
Result: 77.7 percent of pre-pandemic ridership, growing 4.1 percent year over year.
Inputs: FTA NTD Complete Monthly Ridership, dataset 8bui-9xvu, data.transportation.gov, fetched August 2, 2026. Same-month comparison avoids seasonal distortion. Calculation: Pioneer Institute.
Source: FTA National Transit Database, Complete Monthly Ridership (SRC-301), through May 2026. Calculation: Pioneer Institute.

Which riders came back first?

The average hides two different recoveries. Each bar is a mode’s May 2026 ridership as a share of its own May 2019 level; the gold line marks a full return.
Figure 2 Recovery vs the same month in 2019, by mode
Commuter rail is the outlier above its 2019 level at 101.3 percent, while heavy rail, the subway that anchors the system, sits at 66.1 percent (derived vs same month 2019, SRC-301). Bus, bus rapid transit, and ferry all cluster near ninety percent. The RIDE, the paratransit service, is at 64.8 percent. Read together: the suburban, occasional-trip services recovered first, and the everyday urban core is what remains missing.
Source: FTA NTD Complete Monthly Ridership (SRC-301), May 2026 vs May 2019, derived. Calculation: Pioneer Institute.
▶How this data was rebuilt and verified

Ridership was rebuilt from the FTA National Transit Database Complete Monthly Ridership file (dataset 8bui-9xvu, data.transportation.gov), fetched August 2, 2026, and verified against the recovered legacy DataLabs extract: 1,174 of 1,176 overlapping values matched exactly, and the two recent contracted-bus months that differed were revised upward by the federal source. Ridership figures on this page are therefore verified; cost and farebox figures on the Costs & fares view remain from the legacy extract and are flagged as pending until they are reconciled against NTD annual data.

Sources: FTA NTD Complete Monthly Ridership (SRC-301); recovered legacy DataLabs extract (LEG-MBTA-01). Verification: Pioneer Institute, August 2, 2026.

What does a trip cost to provide?

Fares are what riders pay; these figures are what the service costs to operate, per trip taken. They come from the recovered legacy extract and carry a caution: the reporting year was not recorded, so they are published here flagged as pending until reconciled against federal annual data.
Cost figures are pending verification. Cost per trip and farebox recovery come from the recovered legacy extract (LEG-MBTA-01); the reporting year was not recorded and will be confirmed against NTD annual agency data. Ridership figures on this page are not affected; they are verified against the federal monthly file (SRC-301).
Figure 3 Operating cost per unlinked trip, by mode
Heavy rail is the cheapest service to provide per rider at $4.80 a trip, and commuter rail the most expensive of the fixed-route modes at $19.51 (LEG-MBTA-01). The RIDE, the door-to-door paratransit service, costs $161.25 per trip and is left off the scale so the other modes stay readable. Contracted services are marked; they are operated for the MBTA by private providers. Cost figures are pending verification.
Source: recovered legacy DataLabs extract (LEG-MBTA-01), annual table, reporting year to be confirmed against FTA NTD annual agency data.

What share of the cost do fares cover?

Farebox recovery is the share of operating cost paid by the people riding. Every point below one hundred is covered by taxpayers, which is what makes this the central policy number in any debate about fares, service, and subsidy.
Figure 4 Farebox recovery, by mode
No MBTA mode comes close to paying for itself at the farebox. Ferry leads at 35.8 percent and heavy rail follows at 33.7 percent; bus riders cover 12.3 percent of the cost of their trips, and The RIDE covers 1.8 percent (LEG-MBTA-01). Cost figures are pending verification.
Source: recovered legacy DataLabs extract (LEG-MBTA-01), annual table, reporting year to be confirmed.
▶What this page does not cover

This tool covers MBTA ridership as unlinked passenger trips, service levels, and per-trip cost and farebox recovery by mode. It does not cover safety, reliability or on-time performance, the capital budget, debt, the fares charged to riders, or other transit agencies. Questions on those subjects asked through the DataLabs front door are declined rather than improvised, and every one is logged and informs what gets verified and added next.
