How funded is the Massachusetts Teachers retirement system?

58.7%

Massachusetts · January 1, 2023 valuation · SRC-501

Mass Teachers (MTRS) was 58.7 percent funded on its January 1, 2023 valuation, rank 93 of 105 (SRC-501). The State Retirement Board was 71.3 percent funded (SRC-501). Across 105 boards the dollar-weighted funded ratio was 66.4 percent (derived, SRC-501). State and Teacher retirees were paid $6.63 billion in calendar 2025 (SRC-503).

How do the 105 boards sit?

Each bar is a count of boards in a funded-ratio band. The State Retirement Board and Mass Teachers sit in the table, not as a wall of 105 bars. Four boards sit at or above 100 percent; thirteen sit below 60 percent.
Figure 1 Funded ratio across Massachusetts retirement boards
Funded ratio is actuarial assets divided by actuarial accrued liability, as of each board's latest valuation date (SRC-501). Most boards in this file are January 1, 2022 or January 1, 2023. A ratio of 100 percent is fully funded. The dollar-weighted ratio across all 105 boards is not the unweighted mean of the bars: the State and Teacher systems dominate the dollars.
How this is calculated
Funded ratio (PERAC, checked by Pioneer)
funded ratio = 1 - unfunded actuarial liability / actuarial accrued liability The published PERAC ratio and this recompute match within 0.6 percentage points on every board. UAL and AAL in the extract are rounded, so a tenth of a point of gap is expected on small boards.
Result: the percentages on this page are PERAC's published funded ratios, with the recompute as the verification path.
Inputs: compiled PERAC board valuations in the Performance extract (SRC-501). Calculation of ranks and the dollar-weighted ratio: Pioneer Institute.
Source: PERAC funded ratios and board valuation reports (SRC-501). Calculation: Pioneer Institute (ranks and the dollar-weighted ratio).

How has funded status moved?

The State Retirement Board and Mass Teachers, year by year, against one selected board. Gaps in a line are years with no new valuation on file.
Figure 2 Funded ratio, State, Mass Teachers, and selected board
State funded status fell from the mid-80s in the mid-2000s to the mid-60s after the financial crisis, then recovered into the low 70s on the 2022 and 2023 valuations (SRC-501). Mass Teachers has stayed below 60 percent for most of the last decade. Select another board to put it on the same axis.
Source: PERAC board actuarial valuations, compiled (SRC-501). Calculation: Pioneer Institute.

What did each board earn in 2023?

One-year investment return, calendar 2023, from the compiled PERAC returns file. The State and Teacher systems both printed 11.66 percent. The 2024 Investment Report is the next vintage and is not yet in this ledger.

Returns are time-weighted investment performance as compiled by PERAC (SRC-502). They are not the assumed rate of return used in the valuation (that assumption is in the table, typically 7.00 percent for the large systems). Five-year, ten-year, and since-inception returns are in the table.

How large is the State and Teacher retiree payroll?

Annual pensions paid to named MSERS and MTRS retirees, calendar years 2011 through 2025. This is the Comptroller's CTHRU file, not a PERAC actuarial headcount: it does not include survivors counted in the valuation census, and it does not cover local-board retirees.
Figure 4 Annual pensions paid to State and Teacher retirees
In 2025 the file has 66,071 MSERS retirees paid $3.01 billion and 69,843 MTRS retirees paid $3.62 billion (SRC-503). Combined annual pensions rose 84.2 percent from 2011. Name search uses the 2026 CTHRU file (year-to-date), 133,563 named retirees (SRC-503).
Source: CTHRU State and Teachers Retirement Benefits, Office of the Comptroller (SRC-503). Calculation: Pioneer Institute (yearly sums and ranks).

▶What this page does not cover

This tool publishes board funded status and returns, the compiled State and Teacher retiree payroll, and a last-name search of the latest CTHRU year for MSERS and MTRS. It does not look up a named retiree in the ask box (use the search on the Retirees view), cover local-board retiree names, estimate a future benefit, or forecast funded status. PERAC's 2024 Investment Report and the July 2026 funded-ratios table are newer than the board side of this ledger and are flagged pending. Questions on excluded subjects asked through the DataLabs front door are declined rather than improvised.
